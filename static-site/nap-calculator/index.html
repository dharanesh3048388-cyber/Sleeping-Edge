Choosing a nap duration

Brief naps of around 10–20 minutes are commonly used when the goal is a short rest with less chance of waking from deeper sleep. Individual responses vary, and no duration guarantees that you will avoid sleep inertia.

Longer naps provide more time for sleep but can leave some people groggy after waking. A 90-minute option is not a promise of one complete sleep cycle because sleep architecture and cycle length vary.

Nap timing also matters: a late nap may make it harder for some people to fall asleep at their usual bedtime. If you regularly need long daytime naps or struggle to stay awake, consider speaking with a healthcare professional.
