What does research say about noise and sleep?

People use continuous sound for different reasons, including masking an unpredictable room noise or creating a familiar bedtime environment. That does not mean a particular colour of noise reliably improves sleep for everyone. A 2020 systematic review judged the evidence for continuous white or similar broadband noise to be very low quality, with findings ranging from improved to disrupted sleep. A 2022 review of white noise, pink noise and mixed audio also concluded that strong evidence was lacking.

A 2025 perspective review likewise described findings for ambient sounds such as white and pink noise as inconclusive. SleepingEdge therefore presents these sounds as optional listening choices—not treatments, brainwave tools, or guaranteed sleep aids.

Use sound gently

Start at a low volume and use only as much as you find comfortable. Turn it down or stop if it becomes distracting, masks important alerts, or causes discomfort. Sound is optional; silence is also a valid choice. If sleep problems persist, speak with a qualified health professional rather than relying on an audio tool.
