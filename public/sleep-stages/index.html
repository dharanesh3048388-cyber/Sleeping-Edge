The two broad phases of sleep

Sleep alternates between non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep and rapid eye movement (REM) sleep. NREM has three stages, commonly labelled N1, N2 and N3. N1 is the transition from wakefulness; N2 is established light sleep; and N3 is deep or slow-wave sleep. REM is a distinct phase associated with vivid brain activity and temporary muscle atonia.

How sleep changes through the night

Sleep usually moves through repeated NREM and REM phases. The NHLBI describes cycles that commonly recur about every 80–100 minutes, with four to six cycles in a typical night. Deep NREM sleep is usually more prominent earlier in the night, while REM periods often become longer later. The pattern changes with age and varies from person to person.

Source: National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute — How Sleep Works: Sleep Phases and Stages.

Why a sleep-cycle calculator cannot detect a stage

A clock-based calculator can add or subtract an assumed interval from a bedtime or wake time. It cannot tell whether you are in N1, N2, N3 or REM at a particular moment. Sleep studies use measurements such as brain activity and eye movements to classify sleep. A predicted time is therefore not a measurement of your personal sleep architecture.

Sleep duration still comes first

Do not shorten sleep simply to target a predicted cycle boundary. Many adults need at least seven hours, and needs vary by age and individual. A regular schedule with adequate sleep opportunity is a more sensible starting point than trying to time an exact stage.
