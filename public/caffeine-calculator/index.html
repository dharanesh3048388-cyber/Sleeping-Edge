What the caffeine calculator estimates

Choose a beverage preset or enter a caffeine amount, record when you consumed it, set your planned bedtime and adjust the assumed caffeine half-life. The calculator estimates the amount that may remain at bedtime and the time required to fall below an illustrative 25 mg reference amount.

Important: 25 mg is only a model reference

It is not a clinically established safe threshold, a medical cutoff or a guarantee that caffeine below this amount will not affect your sleep.

How the calculation works

The tool uses the standard first-order decay equation: estimated caffeine remaining = starting dose × 0.5 raised to (elapsed hours ÷ assumed half-life). Its default half-life is 5.5 hours, but actual caffeine metabolism varies. The estimate is sensitive to the dose and time you enter as well as the half-life selected.

Why a universal caffeine cut-off is misleading

Research shows that dose and timing both matter. A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis found that caffeine can reduce total sleep time and estimated that a standard coffee may need to be consumed many hours before bedtime to reduce that effect. A 2024 randomized crossover study in 23 men found different effects for 100 mg and 400 mg doses, illustrating why one fixed cut-off should not be treated as universal advice. Individual response can differ from study averages.

Sources: 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis · 2024 randomized crossover study.

How to use it responsibly

  1. Choose the closest beverage preset or enter the best available caffeine-dose estimate.
  2. Enter the time you consumed it and your intended bedtime.
  3. Adjust the half-life assumption to compare scenarios; do not treat one setting as your measured metabolism.
  4. Interpret the remaining-milligram result as an estimate, not a safety assessment.

Caffeine content varies by product, serving size and preparation. The calculator cannot verify a drink's actual caffeine content or account for every medication or health factor. For personalised advice, ask a qualified healthcare professional.
