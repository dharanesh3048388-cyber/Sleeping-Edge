What the seven-day tracker measures

Enter your own daily sleep target and the hours you slept on each of the previous seven days. SleepingEdge adds the reported hours, compares them with seven times your chosen daily target and displays any arithmetic shortfall. It also reports your average sleep across the week.

The formula

Weekly shortfall = max(0, (daily target × 7) − total reported sleep). For example, a 7.5-hour daily target represents 52.5 hours across seven days. If the hours entered total 49.5, the arithmetic shortfall is 3 hours. This is a simple comparison against your own target, not a clinical measurement of sleep debt.

Why the number is not a repayment prescription

Sleep loss is not a bank balance that can always be repaid hour for hour on a chosen schedule. The calculation does not measure sleep quality, sleep stages, circadian timing or individual recovery. It also cannot determine whether your chosen target is appropriate for your age or health.

Most adults need at least seven hours of sleep, but sleep needs vary by age and individual. Use a suitable target and focus on allowing enough regular sleep rather than trying to eliminate a score. See the CDC sleep recommendations for general guidance.

Chronotype is a tendency, not a diagnosis

The companion rhythm view describes broad earlier, intermediate, later and variable timing tendencies. It does not measure your circadian rhythm or determine an ideal bedtime. Work, light exposure, health and daily responsibilities can all affect sleep timing.

How to use it

  1. Choose a daily sleep target that is realistic and appropriate for you.
  2. Enter actual sleep duration for each of the seven days.
  3. Review the weekly total and average, not just the shortfall.
  4. Use the result to reflect on your schedule; seek professional advice if persistent sleep problems or daytime impairment concern you.
