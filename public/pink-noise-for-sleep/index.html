What is pink noise?

Pink noise has less power at higher frequencies, following an approximate 1/f spectral relationship. In simplified terms, it distributes energy more evenly across octave bands than white noise. It often sounds fuller and less hiss-like. The SleepingEdge preset is synthesized locally in your browser.

Pink noise versus white noise

White noise has a flatter power spectrum per unit of frequency and can sound brighter. Pink noise reduces high-frequency energy relative to low frequencies, so some people prefer its warmer texture. Neither sound is automatically better for sleep; comfort and the sound environment matter.

Does pink noise deepen sleep or improve memory?

Those claims should be treated cautiously. Some experiments have investigated timed auditory stimulation and sleep-related brain activity, but that is not the same as showing that an ordinary continuous pink-noise generator improves deep sleep or memory for everyday users. A systematic review of auditory stimulation in adults concluded that strong evidence for white or pink noise as a sleep intervention was lacking. A 2025 review also described evidence for ambient noise as inconclusive.

Who might prefer pink noise?

Someone who finds white noise too bright may simply prefer pink noise's lower-frequency-weighted sound. There is no reliable way to predict that preference in advance. Compare it with white noise and brown noise, or choose silence.

Keep expectations realistic

Pink noise is not a treatment for insomnia, anxiety or any medical condition. It cannot identify or control your sleep stages. Stop using it if it becomes distracting or uncomfortable.
