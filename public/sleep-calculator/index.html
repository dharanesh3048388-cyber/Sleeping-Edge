What the sleep calculator can estimate

  • Bedtime from a wake-up time: choose when you need to wake and compare suggested times to go to bed.
  • Wake-up times from a bedtime: enter a planned bedtime and compare several possible wake times.
  • Timing from now: use the current device time as the starting point and see illustrative wake-time options.

How the calculation works

The calculator uses a simple planning equation: estimated time in bed equals the selected number of assumed cycles multiplied by the cycle-length setting, plus a sleep-onset buffer. The default cycle interval is 90 minutes and the default sleep-onset buffer is 14 minutes; both are assumptions that can be adjusted in the settings. The tool compares three to six assumed cycles.

This model is intentionally transparent, but it is not a physiological prediction. Sleep cycles are not identical, do not run at a fixed length throughout the night, and vary between people and nights. The NHLBI describes typical cycles as recurring about every 80–100 minutes, while noting that sleep patterns change across the night.

Do not sacrifice sleep duration to chase a cycle

A suggested wake time is not automatically a healthy one. Many adults need at least seven hours of sleep, and some need more. Choose a schedule that allows enough sleep for your age and circumstances before comparing cycle-based estimates. See the CDC sleep guidance for age-related recommendations.

How to use the calculator

  1. Open the calculator and choose the mode that matches your question.
  2. Enter the wake-up or bedtime time, or use the current-time option.
  3. Review the estimated options and their stated sleep-duration assumptions.
  4. Adjust sleep-onset latency or cycle length in settings if you want to compare a different model.

Common questions

Is a 90-minute sleep cycle exact?

No. Ninety minutes is a convenient default setting, not a universal biological constant. Actual sleep cycles vary within and between nights.

Does this calculator know when I am in deep or REM sleep?

No. It uses clock arithmetic only. It does not monitor brain activity or detect sleep stages. A sleep study uses physiological measurements to classify sleep.

Is the result a medical recommendation?

No. It is a planning estimate. Persistent sleep difficulty, excessive daytime sleepiness or concerns about a sleep disorder should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional.
